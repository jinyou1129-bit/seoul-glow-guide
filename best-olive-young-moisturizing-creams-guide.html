Find your winter texture

Rich Barrier Balm

Thick, cushiony textures feel protective and often include emollients that help reduce moisture loss. Best for dry patches or cold, windy days; use a thinner layer where needed.

Gel-Cream

Water-rich and lightweight, gel-creams can feel refreshing on combination or oily skin. If skin still feels tight in winter, add a richer layer to dry areas.

Fluid Lotion

A pourable, lighter option for quick application and easy layering, especially on the body. How moisturizing it feels depends on the formula, not its name alone.

After gentle cleansing, apply to slightly damp skin. In the morning finish with broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher. Patch-test new formulas, introduce one at a time, and stop if irritation occurs. Persistent or severe symptoms deserve a dermatologist’s advice.

Frequently asked questions

Which cream is best for very dry winter skin?

Aestura Atobarrier 365 and Illiyoon Ceramide Ato are richer options with barrier-supporting lipids. Individual tolerance varies, so patch-test and check the label.

What is the difference between a gel-cream and a barrier cream?

Gel-creams tend to feel lighter and water-rich; richer barrier creams often contain more emollients to help reduce moisture loss. Texture alone does not determine effectiveness.

Can oily or acne-prone skin use moisturizer?

Yes. A lightweight gel-cream may feel comfortable, but no product is guaranteed to prevent breakouts. Dr.G is a soothing option, not acne medication.

How do I apply moisturizer in winter?

After gentle cleansing, apply moisturizer to slightly damp skin. Add more to dry areas as needed; use broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher during the day.

Are all seven available at every Olive Young?

Availability, packaging and formulas vary by location and date. The exact Purito B5 Re-Barrier Cream listing was not confirmed; ask staff to check stock and the current ingredient label.

Can I take cream on a flight?

Creams are generally treated as liquids or gels. Check the current carry-on container limits and bag rules for your airport and airline; larger containers may need to go in checked baggage.
